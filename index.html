Conjuncts and sound changes

Bangla is not read letter by letter, so the page applies the common spoken rules.

  • The inherent vowel of অ is o in Bangla (কলম = kolom). Switch to Hindi-style to see a instead (kalam).
  • A consonant at the end of a word is silent-vowelled: নাম = naam. A word ending in a conjunct keeps the vowel: বন্ধ = bondho.
  • ্য and ্ব after a consonant double it: সত্য = shotto, বিশ্ব = bissho. At the start of a word ্য sounds like ae.
  • স is s before ক খ ট ঠ ত থ প ফ (স্টেশন = steshon) and sh everywhere else. With dotted letters on, শ is ś and ষ is ṣ, while স stays sh or s by sound.
  • র্য and র্ব do not double (সূর্য = shoorjo), হ্ন হ্ম হ্ল swap to nh, mh, lh (চিহ্ন = chinho), and ত্ম দ্ম গ্ম double the first letter (পদ্ম = poddo).
  • There is only a tiny exception list (শুভ = shubho), not a dictionary, so a few words with irregular vowel colouring (for example এক said as aek) will come out in their regular form.
